Scribe can save a dictation as a text note or transcribe a voice recording you already have. Both run on the Mac without uploading audio; dictations become notes, while imported recordings appear under Meetings in the Library.

## How do I capture a new thought as text?

Click into a text field, hold the talk shortcut and speak, then release it to insert the words. Everything you dictate is also saved as a note in the Library unless history is off. This gives you a copy to return to after dictating into another app.

Open the Library by choosing **Open Scribe…** (<kbd>⌘O</kbd>) from the menu bar. Notes are grouped under **Pinned**, **Today**, **Yesterday** and **Earlier**, with a search field at the top. Open the saved dictation to edit it.

You can also create a note without dictating: choose **New Note** (<kbd>⌘N</kbd>) to type one, or **Paste as New Note** (<kbd>⇧⌘V</kbd>) to save text from the clipboard. The [Library guide](/docs/library) describes these controls. On Mac, the documented workflow is to capture dictation and then open its saved note to edit it.

## Can I tidy the text after I speak it?

Open the note and use the presets along the bottom of the editor: **Refine**, **Proofread**, **Shorter**, **Clearer**, **Friendlier**, **Professional**, **Bullets** and **Translate**. A preset acts on selected text, or the whole note if nothing is selected.

Use Proofread for spelling, grammar and punctuation, or Bullets to turn the thought into a list. Presets work on the current text, so you can apply another one afterwards. Each result is one undo step. If rewriting fails, the text stays unchanged. [Refine](/docs/refine) explains the engines and each preset.

Turn on **Show changes** to compare the edited text with the original dictation. The comparison is read-only while it is shown. Edits save automatically; **Save as Copy** keeps the original note and opens a separate copy. **Copy** and **Share** let you use the result elsewhere.

## How do I transcribe a recording I already have?

In the Library, click the arrow next to **Record**, choose **Transcribe a Recording…**, then pick the file. You can also drag an audio file from the Finder onto the Library window. Supported formats are **m4a, mp3, wav, aac, caf and aiff**.

Transcription uses your dictation languages. If Scribe says **The meeting language isn't installed**, add the language under **Settings › Languages** and download the language pack if that language needs it.

The file is processed as a meeting. Its title starts as the file name without its extension, and its date starts as the file's creation date. Both can be changed. [Transcribe a recording](/docs/transcribe-a-recording) covers importing and the processing steps.

## Where do the transcript and summary appear?

Open the imported recording under **Meetings**, below the notes in the Library sidebar. Processing shows **Saving audio**, **Transcribing**, **Identifying speakers** if the model is installed, and **Summarising**.

Speaker labels need the optional **13.7 MB** speaker model. Summaries need an available Refine engine: Apple Intelligence works in chunks, while Gemma 4 summarises the whole transcript in one pass. Very short recordings show **Too short to summarise**.

The meeting contains the transcript, a summary of up to six bullets and a checklist of action items with owners where they were named. Double-click a transcript turn to correct it, then choose **Update summary** if needed. Copy Summary, Transcript or Everything, or export as Markdown or plain text. See [Meetings on Mac](/docs/meetings).

## What happens to the original audio?

Scribe copies an imported file into its meeting storage and leaves the original untouched. The copy is deleted once the transcript exists unless **Keep recordings' audio** is on in **Settings › Meetings**. Turn that on before importing if you want playback in Scribe.

## How do I keep a dictated note?

**Keep history**, under **Settings › History & Privacy**, applies to untouched dictations. A note you edit or pin stays until you delete it. With history off, dictations are not saved to the Library. Each device keeps its own Library; there is no automatic sync.
