## What a snippet is

A snippet is a short trigger phrase paired with fixed text. Say the phrase and the text goes in verbatim: your postal address, an email sign-off, a link you paste ten times a day, a standard reply.

## Add a snippet

1. Open **Settings › Snippets** (Mac or iPhone).
2. Enter the **phrase** you will say, for example *my address*.
3. Enter the **text** to insert, with line breaks if you need them.
4. Save.

Pick phrases you would not say by accident. Two or three words work well.

## Use one while dictating

- **On its own.** Hold Talk, say *my address*, let go. The snippet is inserted as written; Refine is skipped so nothing is reworded.
- **Inside a sentence.** Say *Send it to my address, thanks*. The phrase is replaced by the snippet text in the final result and the rest of the sentence is refined as usual.

Matching ignores case and small differences in punctuation.

## Edit or remove

Back in Settings › Snippets, select a snippet to change its phrase or text, or delete it. Changes apply to the next dictation.

## Where snippets live

On the device. The iPhone app shares them with its keyboard; Mac and iPhone keep separate lists. A [backup](/docs/backup) includes Snippets.

## Common problems

**The snippet was reworded.** It was recognised inside a longer sentence and Refine changed the words around it, not the snippet itself. If the inserted text is wrong, check the snippet text in Settings.

**It did not trigger.** Say the phrase exactly as written. Phrases that are also common words, like *thanks*, are easy to miss; make them more distinctive.
